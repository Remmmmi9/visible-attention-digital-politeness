My Role & Reflection

How I helped shape, organise and reflect on the visual podcast, from early topic development to final production.

Conversation does not need to be perfect.

01 Planning 02 Language Pressure 03 Read Receipts 04 Editing & Ethics 05 Final Reflection
01

Planning, Organising and Moving the Project Forward

I was involved in this project from the first discussion to the final submission. Looking back, my role was not only about “making content”. I was also helping the project move forward and keeping the overall structure clear.

At the beginning, our direction was mainly about comparing the functional differences between WeChat and WhatsApp. Later, we realised that this was not really what we wanted to discuss. If we only focused on platform features, the podcast would become quite surface-level and leave less space for deeper discussion.

After that, I suggested focusing on read receipts and reply pressure. I wanted to move the topic away from platform functions and towards people’s emotions and behaviour in digital communication.

In the early stage, our group stayed in the “idea discussion” stage for quite a long time. I gradually realised that without a clearer structure and plan, the project would be difficult to move forward. So I started organising checklists, script structures and timelines, hoping to make it clearer what we needed to do next and how we could move step by step into recording and post-production.

02

Language Pressure and Group Communication

This project felt special to me because it was built around conversation and real-time communication. Whether in class or in group work, it required a lot of talking, responding and explaining.

As a non-native speaker, I often feel that I already have ideas in my mind, but I cannot always express them as quickly or as fully as I want. In group discussions, I sometimes worry that I cannot keep up with the rhythm, or that I cannot respond to others naturally enough.

Because of this, I really wanted to confirm the script and structure before recording. I know some people feel more comfortable with spontaneous conversation, but for me, this kind of podcast conversation was quite stressful. I wanted to prepare my part more fully because I did not want to waste everyone’s recording time, or make the discussion unclear because I could not express myself well.

After we started recording, I slowly realised that conversation does not need to be completely perfect. Real discussion naturally includes pauses, mistakes and unfinished sentences. My group members were also very understanding and supportive, which helped me relax a lot. This project helped me become more confident in expressing myself in English within a collaborative environment.

I was not only making content. I was helping the project move forward.
03

Rethinking Read Receipts and Reply Pressure

While discussing read receipts and reply pressure, I started to rethink my own everyday habits on social media and messaging apps.

In China, people usually use WeChat. Although WeChat does not have the same visible read receipt function as WhatsApp, people still care about why someone has not replied. Even without a “seen” signal, people may still wonder:

  • What is the other person doing now?
  • Why have they not replied?
  • Do they not want to reply?

Because of this, many people naturally add a small explanation when they reply, such as “I was busy just now” or “I only just saw this”.

Before starting this project, I also asked some friends how they would feel if a message had already been marked as “seen” on WhatsApp, but they were not ready to reply yet. Many of them said their first reaction would be awkwardness or uncertainty.

One friend’s answer stayed with me. She said she usually does not open the message directly. Instead, she first reads it through the notification preview, and only opens the chat when she is ready to reply.

I found this habit interesting because it shows how platform design can slowly shape people’s behaviour. Users do not simply accept platform features passively. They also develop small strategies to manage pressure.

When I first started using WhatsApp, I also replied quickly because of the read receipt function. I felt that if the other person could see I was online, or knew I had read the message, not replying might seem impolite or make it look like I did not care enough. Sometimes, even before I had decided what to say, I would send a quick message like, “I’ll reply to you later.”

After gradually getting used to the platform, I became less anxious about it. I started to realise that other people may not notice or care about “seen” as much as I imagined. When the people around me treated this feature as part of a normal communication environment, I also became more relaxed.

04

Editing, Ethics and Public-facing Work

In the final stage of the project, I helped organise references, reading citations and media credits. I wanted the visual podcast to be supported not only by personal experience, but also by module readings and relevant research.

During the editing process, I also realised that visual editing is not only an aesthetic issue. Small details such as close-up shots, subtitles, transitions and background visuals all affect how the audience experiences the conversation. They can make the podcast feel more intimate, more awkward or more pressured. In other words, editing also participates in meaning-making.

A small feature can change how people feel.
05

Final Reflection

If I were to do this project again, I would still choose this topic because it is closely connected to everyday life.

If I had the chance to start again, I would like to explore the idea of “habitual environments” more deeply. Often, we are not only shaped by platform features themselves. We also slowly adapt to certain ways of communicating through long-term use.

I would also tell myself: relax a little.

At the beginning of the project, I was afraid that I would not express myself well enough. I was afraid of making mistakes, and I worried that I might slow the team down. Later, I realised that what really matters is not perfect expression, but honest communication and collaboration.
