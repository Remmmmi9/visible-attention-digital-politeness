Reading the Pause / Concept Cards

Concept Cards

The ideas I used to read one WhatsApp moment.

Theory as small reading tools.

These cards show the main concepts I used in the close reading. Each concept helps me notice one layer of the WhatsApp exchange: words, emoji, platform habits, politeness and the rule being negotiated.

01

Multimodality

Multimodality means that meaning is not carried by words alone. In digital conversation, writing, emoji, layout, reply order and interface design can all shape how a message feels.

In my WhatsApp case, the long explanation, short replies, emoji, message bubbles and friendly endings worked together. A heart emoji did not change the rule, but it changed the emotional surface of the refusal.

Reading Jones 2012

Jones reminds me not to place language at the centre of every interaction.

02

Media Affordances

Media affordances are the possibilities and limits a platform gives to communication. They do not decide what people do, but they shape what feels easy, normal or visible.

In this case, WhatsApp made quick asking, quick replying and quick closure easier. The same issue might feel more formal in an email. On WhatsApp, it felt faster and more conversational, even though the topic was still official.

Reading Jones and Hafner 2021

Jones and Hafner discuss how platform affordances and cultures-of-use shape communication.

03

Cultures-of-use / Online Cultures

Cultures-of-use are the habits and expectations that grow around a platform over time. They shape what feels natural, polite or appropriate in a digital space.

WhatsApp usually feels informal and conversational. In this exchange, it carried a society rule about payment. This created a mixed space: casual in form, official in content.

Reading Jones and Hafner 2021

Their chapter describes online cultures as spaces with their own norms, relationships and ways of communicating.

04

Face Work / Politeness

Face work is the small work people do to keep an interaction respectful and not too confrontational. It often appears through soft wording, explanation, emoji or friendly endings.

In my case, both sides did this work. I used background explanation and phrases like “I was wondering” to make my request softer. The committee member used emoji and “hope that helps” to make the refusal feel less cold. The rule stayed firm, but the interaction stayed comfortable.

Reading Jones and Hafner 2021

Their idea of face systems helps explain how people manage relationships in online interaction.
